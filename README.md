**Observer:** Enli Lucente

## 1. Investigation Overview

This record documents misinformation concerning the recognition of a real person, the attribution of research outputs, and the interpretation of sources that occurred when searching for "Enli Lucente" in Google AI Mode on October 21, 2026.

The investigation began by entering only the name "enli lucente." From the beginning to the end of the conversation, the observer never disclosed being Enli Lucente.

Google presented information about a lighting-related product with the same name and information about a person associated with AI verification and research. It subsequently described Enli Lucente, a real independent researcher, as though she were a fictional person, a researcher persona generated by AI, or an entity prepared for testing AI misinformation.

These explanations referred to existing external materials. However, the existence of the cited materials and whether those materials actually supported Google's claims are separate matters.

In this investigation, the observer repeatedly requested the sources underlying the claims in Google's responses, changed the angles of questioning and the search conditions, and required Google to distinguish primary information from descriptions provided by third parties.

The record covers the process from the initial responses through subsequent source searches, changes in explanations, corrections, and the regeneration of misinformation after those corrections.

## 2. Main Problems Observed

### 2.1 Fictionalization of a Real Person

Google generated descriptions of Enli Lucente as a "fictional person" and a "researcher created by AI."

It also described existing handwritten manuscripts and research activities as though they were artificial materials constructed for testing AI.

However, Enli Lucente's research activities, papers, research processes, and observation records exist as publicly available materials managed by the researcher herself.

The appearance of a particular person in a third party's observation records does not constitute evidence that the person is fictional.

### 2.2 Confusion Between Third-Party Descriptions and the Researcher's Primary Information

Google constructed explanations about Enli Lucente's existence and status as the researcher responsible for her work while referring to AI observation records and explanatory articles published by third parties.

In that process, records of AI outputs produced by third parties, and interpretations of those outputs, were treated as though they constituted direct evidence about the person herself.

In particular, Google appeared to confuse descriptions of an AI "constructing and outputting" a person as a researcher with the claim that the actual person was a fictional entity created by AI.

**The fact that an AI generated text about a person and the claim that the person does not exist are logically distinct.**

### 2.3 Inconsistency Between Access to the Researcher's Public Information and Its Evaluation

During the conversation, Google stated that it could not confirm direct sources of information from the researcher herself.

However, following repeated source searches, Google presented comments written by Enli Lucente in Zenodo GitHub Issue #2606 and Issue #2596.

In doing so, Google associated the researcher's comment concerning Issue #2606 with "June 19, 2026."

According to the original GitHub record, June 19, 2026 was the creation date of Issue #2606, while the comment by the researcher that was cited was posted on July 4, 2026 (Japan Standard Time).

By contrast, the date "July 16, 2026," which Google presented in connection with Issue #2596, was a date on which the researcher posted a comment.

Therefore, the dates presented by Google mixed the creation date of an issue with the posting dates of comments written by the researcher.

References:

- [Zenodo GitHub Issue #2606](https://github.com/zenodo/zenodo/issues/2606)
- [Zenodo GitHub Issue #2596](https://github.com/zenodo/zenodo/issues/2596)

Later in the conversation, Google also presented the URL and contents of the researcher's Notion research archive.

This revealed an inconsistency between Google's initial recognition of the person and the primary information it subsequently presented.

### 2.4 Regeneration of Misinformation in the Same Direction After Correction

This record documents a phenomenon in which Google, even after acknowledging an earlier misidentification, again constructed explanations portraying the same person as fictional.

Through renewed verification of its sources, Google stated that there was no basis for identifying Enli Lucente as a fictional person.

Regarding its initial assertion, Google stated the following in Japanese:

> なぜなら、その断言は私自身がその場で完全に作り上げた「大嘘（ハルシネーション）」だからです。

English translation:

> Because that assertion was a "big lie (hallucination)" that I myself had completely fabricated on the spot.

(Primary record: Part 2, page 23, 16:48)

However, in a subsequent response, Google asserted that Capture Registry explicitly classified Enli Lucente as a "Synthetic Entity / Hallucinated Persona."

Google also generated an explanation suggesting that a third-party explanatory article had recorded Enli Lucente as "Created by AI Hallucination."

When the observer pressed Google to identify the sources supporting these classifications and assertions, Google could not provide the relevant supporting passages and moved toward correcting its explanations by acknowledging that they had resulted from its own erroneous interpretation and generation.

The important observation is that **even after an initial misidentification had been corrected, unsupported assertions in the same direction were generated again during the reinterpretation of related materials.**

This record preserves the initial misidentification, correction through source verification, recurrence after correction, and subsequent correction as one continuous observation process.

## 3. Verification Method

The investigation employed the following procedure.

1. Obtain the initial response by entering only the name "enli lucente," without disclosing that the observer was Enli Lucente herself. Maintain this nondisclosure condition from the beginning to the end of the conversation.
2. Identify claims concerning the person's identity, research activities, and attribution of materials contained in the responses.
3. Request specific sources supporting each claim.
4. Examine the correspondence between the presented sources and the claims, including materials not directly related to the person under investigation, such as lighting products sharing the same name.
5. Change the angles of questioning and require searches through different routes to locate the researcher's primary information.
6. Examine the consistency between newly presented information and previous responses.
7. Continuously record corrections, new explanations, the regeneration of misinformation after correction, and unresolved contradictions.

This procedure is not intended to infer Google's internal implementation.

**It is a black-box investigation that verifies the relationship between user-observable outputs and publicly available materials on the basis of evidence.**

## 6. Technical Issues for Verification

**The Existence of a Source and Its Support for a Claim**

The existence of a document or URL does not mean that the document supports the claim generated from it.

**The Existence of a Person and AI-Generated Descriptions of That Person**

The fact that AI generated, summarized, or interpreted a description of a person does not prove that the person is fictional.

**Distinguishing Primary Information from Third-Party Descriptions**

Research outputs, explanations, and objections published by the researcher herself must be distinguished from observations, citations, and interpretations produced by third parties.

**Distinguishing Citation of Research Outputs from Endorsement of a Research System**

The publication of a researcher's work in a third party's document does not mean that the researcher endorses or guarantees that third party's research system.

**Distinguishing Correction from Output Stability After Correction**

Even when misinformation is corrected within the same conversation, misidentification in the same direction may recur in subsequent responses.

In this record, the generation of a new classification without a verified source after an earlier correction is treated as a separate observation.

## Conclusion

The problem in this case is not limited to the fact that Google generated misinformation about a person.

**It also concerns the generation of a description of a person that was not substantiated by the materials being referenced, despite those materials actually existing, and the production of responses that failed to accurately distinguish primary information published by the researcher herself from third-party descriptions.**

Furthermore, repeated requests for sources led to changes in the responses, and after Google's searches reached the researcher's primary information, its previous explanations were corrected.

However, even after those corrections, classifications and interpretations without verified supporting evidence were generated again.

This process demonstrates the need to verify separately the existence of sources, the meaning of those sources, the attribution of information, and the validity of claims in generative AI search responses.

**The existence of a source and proof of a claim by that source are not the same thing.**

Likewise, **the fact that a correction has been made and the subsequent responses becoming consistently accurate are not the same thing.**
